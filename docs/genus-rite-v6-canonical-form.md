# Genus Rite V6: Old Edge Grounded, Canonical Form

This contract defines the default Artefato genus for every producer: old-edge reasoning,
directed grounding, and a canonical reader journey with a reusable block grammar.
It is not report-only, but it is also not "anything goes". A skill may translate the
grammar into its vehicle; it may not replace the reader experience with a different
shape and still claim the same genus.

## The Canonical Movement

Every substantial artefact runs this movement before close:

`old-edge draft -> actionable gap gate -> directed grounder -> rewrite with visible delta -> fact-audited close`

The movement is authorial, not just a reviewer checklist. The first draft must already
carry a mentor arc; the gate names lacunas; the grounder answers those lacunas; the
rewrite shows what changed. A final artefact that merely satisfies review dimensions after
incremental correction has not manifested the rite.

## The Canonical Form Grammar

The canonical house form is part of the genus:

1. **Thesis title** - the title says the live conclusion, not only the topic.
2. **Live question / reader context** - open by reconstructing what is at stake for the
   default reader, normally Lucas/Ed, without taxing what he already knows.
3. **Identity and setup** - name the experiment, artefact, decision, commit, case,
   prototype, map, or plan being decided; say exactly what was compared or changed.
4. **Configuration / lineage ledger** - enumerate the relevant arms, configs, predecessors,
   commits, reports, or decisions. Use a numbered ledger when more than one predecessor matters.
5. **Observed result / current read** - separate observation, inference, and recommendation.
6. **Concrete mechanism trace** - include at least one worked case, row, round, diff, path,
   failure mode, before/after, map edge, or interaction trace that shows how the result happened.
7. **Interpretation / teaching** - derive the useful lesson, not just the scoreboard.
8. **Mundo / outside frame** - bring field vocabulary, comparable studies, best practices,
   benchmarks, or industry names where they deepen the reader. Explain fit and mismatch.
9. **Grounding effect** - state what the post-gate grounding changed: claim, caveat, example,
   decision, magnitude, analogy, or next validation.
10. **Unknowns / limits** - mark what remains untested, inferred, local, small-N, judge-dependent,
    or outside scope.
11. **Decision / next validation** - close on what to do next, in the reader's live work.
12. **References / pointers** - when Mundo appears, leave useful references or source pointers.

The section names can vary when the vehicle demands it, but the reader must experience this
same sequence. A compact technical ADR, a topology dump, or a page that jumps from thesis to
implementation without reader context, setup, mechanism, Mundo, and decision is not the same
genus.

## The Canonical Block Palette

The canonical form uses structure to carry thinking. V6 makes that palette default:

- **prose paragraphs** for the arc, reader context, derivation, and interpretation;
- **comparison-table / table** for arms, configurations, alternatives, lineage, or fit/mismatch;
- **metrics-grid / chart** for quantitative results or 3+ comparable magnitudes;
- **derivation** for first-principles reasoning;
- **gap-table** for gate lacunas, open unknowns, caveats, or remaining validation;
- **callout** only for a true one-thing, warning, or caveat that changes action;
- **next-steps-grid** for the closing decision and validation path;
- **bibliography** when external sources, field terms, or best practices are used.

Using only paragraphs when the content has comparisons, results, gaps, and next steps is a
format failure. Using structural blocks as decoration is also a failure. The block must carry
the information shape that the reader needs to scan and reuse.

## Canonical Defaults

A conforming artefact combines:

- a clear live question for Lucas/Ed;
- exact arms/configuration;
- result tables;
- a concrete qualitative case;
- numbered lineage;
- Mundo that repositioned the claim instead of decorating it;
- a visible "grounding effect";
- honest caveats about small-N, judge dependence, and transfer limits;
- a next experiment.

Those are now defaults of the genus. A skill-specific vehicle can translate them, but a
producer cannot skip them by saying "the vehicle is different".

## Skill Translation Rule

This is genus, not `report`, so every producer translates the form:

- a **map** can express setup, lineage, mechanism, Mundo, and decision as graph structure plus
  captions, but it still needs the same ordered reader journey;
- a **plan** can express them as decision context, dependency ledger, mechanism/risk trace,
  outside pattern, unknowns, and next validation;
- a **prototype** can express them as interaction state, before/after behavior, constraints,
  references, and test path;
- a **report/research artefact** should look close to the canonical form: prose plus tables,
  gap table, grounding effect, limits, next steps, and references.

The vehicle may alter surface labels. It may not alter the cognitive and visual grammar so far
that a blind reader would say it was written by a different house.

## Fact-Audit

External sources can position a local result in a family of methods. They do not validate local
effect size, saturation band, ranking, causality, or generalization unless they support that
exact transfer. If Mundo did not validate the local result, the artefact says so and still uses
the outside frame to sharpen the next validation.
